# Restore musical intent before adding machinery

**Aste's reported lack of musical power cannot be explained by one missing effect, insufficient distortion or uniformly conservative ranges.** The current six-product source contains both genuinely broad mechanisms and specific failures of musical intent: Sequence can suppress a fresh keyboard attack, Loop's slow motion repeats the wrong time segment, Impulse exposes an unused Tone control, and several prominent controls or meters hide important dependencies. Other limitations are hypotheses rather than faults: Density's interacting gain laws, Harmonic's level-dependent coloration, and Field's bundled hold behavior require controlled musical comparisons. The creator's negative production experience is substantive evidence; automated success does not override it. This audit reviews baseline `8a603dc`, which is not independently established as the exact installed Cubase build. It preserves production DSP and existing sessions while selecting three isolated investigations. **No new human listening preference, native accessibility result or Cubase validation is claimed.** Completed renders, measurements and checks are owned by the accompanying [evidence record](../docs/research/power/EVIDENCE.md); the [capability matrix](../docs/research/power/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md), [interface review](../docs/research/power/INTERFACE.md) and [ranked backlog](../docs/research/power/BACKLOG.md) make the findings actionable.

The completed audit supplies **267 rendered variants / 534 WAVs**, two
byte-identical exports, six actual-editor images, source-grounded investigations
for every product, and three isolated comparisons. The release-aware Sequence
candidate restores the tested fresh attack; the Loop clock candidate follows
the independent motion oracle; Harmonic's linked-gain gesture changes nonlinear
participation but has source-level-dependent tradeoffs. Separate diagnostics
expose Sequence host-gate and Impulse MIDI buffer-dependence. These are concrete
engineering findings, not musical approval. Start with the
[listening and verification index](../docs/research/power/LISTENING.md) and
[per-instrument findings](../docs/research/power/DSP_FINDINGS.md).

## The evidence gap concerns musical intent

Musical power means reaching contrasting, useful results deliberately, hearing why a gesture changes them, and repeating the result during composition. Sonic range, character and depth must survive level matching; responsiveness and performance must survive actual note and automation gestures; workflow must survive a project reopen. These are separate dimensions. A compressor can exhibit strong gain reduction yet be difficult to shape. A synthesizer can generate a nonzero note at six sample rates yet fail ordinary articulation. A reverb can retain finite state while its display communicates the wrong quantity. The supplied complaint therefore warrants tracing the complete path from a musician's action to the resulting sound, not defending an algorithm because a test calls its coefficients “musically monotonic” ([Sequence tests](../tests/sequence_tests.cpp); [family listening policy](../docs/testing/LISTENING.md)).

The repository has already responded to similar feedback. Density's threshold/makeup mapping was revised for normal track levels, its starting blend subsequently increased, Harmonic's outer contours became shelves, Impulse received predictable defaults and audition, and Loop gained tape recall. These are current behavior, not work this audit newly discovers or proposes. Conversely, prior reports repeatedly mark matched listening, real projects, target-host use and supported-hardware timing as incomplete. A protocol, generated audio file or nonzero activity meter is not a completed musical selection ([track-level revision](../docs/adr/0018-track-level-impact.md); [shelves and blend revision](../docs/adr/0019-tone-shelves-and-direct-blend.md); [usability revision](../docs/UX_REVIEW.md)).

**Stable identifiers do not guarantee compatible sound.** Earlier accepted revisions retained IDs while intentionally changing render behavior. The present assignment is stricter: existing Cubase sessions must not silently adopt an alternative law. Accordingly the audit's experiments remain separately compiled or use explicit existing control settings; no favorable measurement alone authorizes replacing the production graph. No callback allocation, locks, waits, filesystem access, logging, unbounded feedback or misleading latency reporting can be traded for impact. By contrast, one particular CPU percentage, initial zero latency, fixed upward pitch intervals and a small preset set are design decisions that should earn their musical tradeoffs. Field and Loop already have explicit memory-budget exceptions, demonstrating why a general small-state target is not an acoustic law ([real-time requirements](../docs/REALTIME_SAFETY.md); [performance targets](../docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGETS.md); [compatibility history](../docs/adr/0019-tone-shelves-and-direct-blend.md#compatibility)).

## Density and Harmonic hide their operating points

### Density has force, but its controls overlap

Current Density is not the older weak-threshold, half-dry processor. Its source maps Density to a threshold of −18…−46 dB, ratios of 3…60, saturation drive of 1…9 and makeup of +12…+22 dB before Crush modifies participation. The present default is 90% Blend and −7 dB Output. Those facts argue against another unmeasured increase in drive or wet level. What already works is substantial: independent dry/processed paths, detector high-pass, fast attack, nonlinear release, continuous stereo linking and distinct saturation/clipping stages. This can transform transients and body without an additional effect block ([current mapping and graph](../apps/density/density_processor.cpp); [defaults](../apps/density/density_processor.hpp)).

The harder question is how independently a musician can shape those effects. Drive raises the detector input as well as nonlinear excitation. Density and Crush both affect threshold, ratio, makeup and saturation. At high compression ratio, increased input can mostly become more reduction rather than more steady post-compression level; transient and recovery changes remain possible. Release depends on absolute envelope magnitude as well as the nominal time and macro. Output can engage optional Protection, so changing Output to perform a comparison can alter the processing being compared. Crush zero is not a universal clean control because the fixed crush clip remains in the branch. These are causal interactions, not evidence that all settings sound alike ([complete graph](../apps/density/density_processor.cpp); [detailed Density investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/density_harmonic.md)).

The smallest justified next step is a matched sensitivity map of Drive, Crush and Density on sparse percussion, bass and dense material, preserving raw gain reduction, transient/body balance and recovery. The interface already has a clear hero control and first-action explanation; it does not show the effective branch contribution, threshold or saturation operating point. Better explanation and fair comparison could expose capability before a DSP change. If timing proves the limitation, reuse the existing peak/RMS, dual-time and programme-memory candidates rather than invent another detector. Historical rankings changed after fixture isolation and threshold recalibration, so they are not universal sonic verdicts. The retained oversampling finalist was rejected against a provisional whole-instance CPU target, not because its measured alias improvement was inherently undesirable; its value still needs current-graph and listener evidence ([Density research history](../docs/products/density/DSP_RESEARCH.md); [interface evidence](../docs/research/power/INTERFACE.md)).

### Harmonic's color requires both a boost and energy

Harmonic offers real broad contouring: Foundation/Air shelves and Body/Presence bells run serially. Neutral and settled cut paths stay clean. Its distinctive nonlinear behavior is a bounded difference between colored and linear state-variable sections, contributed only by positive-gain bands. **The hero macro is exactly inactive in the initial flat setting**, despite showing a nonzero default value. The footer explains the dependency, but eleven active-looking controls and a small instruction are not equivalent to a clear live engaged/inactive state. At low signal levels, the nonlinear difference becomes small even when a band is boosted; a test at half-scale sine amplitude cannot establish useful color on a quiet track ([production sections](../apps/harmonic/harmonic_processor.cpp); [editor](../apps/harmonic/harmonic_plugin.cpp)).

This makes operating point the highest-value initial Harmonic investigation. Compare the unchanged graph at Input/Output 0/0 against a linked +12/−12 dB gesture, then externally level-match the remaining difference. Include neutral, cut-only and Harmonic-zero controls with sufficient settling and headroom. If the nonlinear identity becomes useful through existing controls, a gain-linked gesture, calibration cue or better starting point is smaller than a new topology. A louder EQ result, a larger residual or stronger H3 alone does not establish that identity. The activity meter reports maximum correction magnitude; it is not a distortion ratio or measurement of newly generated harmonic energy ([gain placement and activity](../apps/harmonic/harmonic_processor.cpp); [Harmonic investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/density_harmonic.md)).

Two deeper issues remain open. The outer linear contours now affect shelf plateaus, but their nonlinear state corrections remain centered bandpass-like sections. A bass-shelf boost need not color all boosted bass equally. Serial order also gives early bands control over later nonlinear drive, making some combinations reinforce or suppress coloration. Those mechanisms warrant corner-versus-plateau and multiband tests before a parallel residual or shelf-specific excitation redesign. The earlier residual topology was rejected partly because it collapsed the intended EQ contour; the audit should retain that failure rather than rediscover it as a stronger-looking macro. Input/output and four simultaneous boosts can also produce output above full-scale while remaining within the core's finite bound, so practical headroom management is part of the musical interface ([historical alternatives](../docs/products/harmonic/DSP_RESEARCH.md); [current cleanup and contour](../apps/harmonic/harmonic_processor.cpp)).

## Sequence and Impulse lose control before sophistication

### Sequence must first honor a fresh note

Sequence's dual polyBLEP oscillator morphs, complementary pulse widths, detune, sine sub and shared-state two-/four-pole weighting constitute a credible monophonic voice. The shared-state filter corrected a documented problem with parallel filters losing body at intermediate morphs. Its built-in sequence exposes all sixteen steps and deliberate gate/accent/slide relationships. None of that justifies the direct-MIDI path's articulation behavior: it uses “envelope still active” as the legato condition, and active includes Release. A new note after all keys were released can therefore change pitch while the amplitude envelope continues dying instead of starting a fresh attack. Existing tests render isolated onsets or sequencer transitions and do not establish the missing keyboard behavior ([MIDI and envelope implementation](../apps/sequence/sequence_processor.cpp); [active-state definition](../apps/sequence/sequence_processor.hpp); [filter decision](../docs/adr/0012-sequence-shared-state-character-filter.md)).

The selected alternative changes that decision only: release-tail activity must not masquerade as a held-note overlap. True legato and sequencer slide remain controls, not collateral damage. Compare separated repeated notes, changing pitches, genuine overlaps and long gaps using both envelope observations and audio. Reliable attacks are a plausible major improvement in playability, but the user must still judge whether the resulting articulation suits the instrument. Even this small correction changes previously rendered MIDI phrases, so it remains isolated until a compatibility decision is approved ([Sequence investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/sequence_impulse.md); [executed experiment evidence](../docs/research/power/EVIDENCE.md)).

A separate executed baseline failure concerns host-sequenced buffer invariance, not MIDI retriggering. At 48 kHz and 120 BPM, 127- versus 511-sample maximum blocks produce opposite gate-release decisions at sample 16,680: reconstructing PPQ from each block origin places phase just above versus just below 0.78. The first audio difference occurs at that same sample. Eight-second default renders differ by stereo RMS 0.0000089791; the fast-automation recipe amplifies the divergence to 0.0352369. A diagnostic-only tiny PPQ offset removes the difference in both pairs, supporting a floating-point boundary cause, but is not an adopted correction. Production and the main harness remain unchanged; the strict existing-host automation test remains a failing gate rather than being marked expected-failure. This is an offline timing defect with demonstrated scope, not a completed Cubase reproduction or proof that a one-sample difference is ordinarily audible ([reproduction and limitations](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/sequence_impulse.md); [evidence ledger](../docs/research/power/EVIDENCE.md)).

After articulation, investigate the Pressure/Weight/contour relationship. Pressure increases mixer drive and filter loading while intermediate compensation attenuates signal; its contour multiplier can request up to 8.25 octaves before the cutoff ceiling. Bright settings can spend much of a note at that ceiling. Velocity and accent act after filtering, so soft/hard playing does not independently change filter excitation or envelope depth; the Accent tooltip's “filter emphasis” overstates the implemented action. These are stronger explanations for limited dynamic expression than simply declaring the oscillators weak. A bounded velocity-to-excitation or contour experiment could be worthwhile later, but it changes the performance law and must beat the existing amplitude-only control after level matching. The current equal-size knob grid, percentage-only Wave presentation and compact step controls can also hide relationships that already exist ([signal path](../apps/sequence/sequence_processor.cpp); [editor review](../docs/research/power/INTERFACE.md)).

### Impulse needs truthful object controls

Impulse has substantial rhythmic capability: independent lengths, visible tri-state patterns, timing, conditions, probability, ratchets and deterministic scheduled variation. Its current schema-3 starter deliberately removes polymetric surprise, and audition makes the eight objects discoverable before playback. The sound engines are distinct constructions, but their depth should not be inferred from having eight names. Kick/Low are swept sines with short noise edges; Body is an inharmonic pair; Burst/Crack combine filtered recurrence noise and a sinusoid; Click/Cut use pulse-derived sources; Metal combines inharmonic phases. All share simple amplitude decay and nonlinear loading. Single-voice retriggering replaces existing state rather than layering tails ([objects and scheduling](../apps/impulse/impulse_processor.cpp); [current starter](../apps/impulse/impulse_plugin.cpp); [eight-object investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/sequence_impulse.md)).

**Low Tone has no effect in its synthesis branch.** Other controls also need accurate expectations: sound values are latched on a hit, so editing a long tail does not necessarily reshape it; Click Tone primarily scales its pulse before saturation rather than directly changing a filter or shape. Several pitch-envelope and filter coefficients are fixed per sample, changing their physical time scales across rates. Metal's mixed term multiplies a wrapped phase by a noninteger ratio, introducing discontinuities. None of these should be disguised by a bigger range or a prettier visualization. The per-object investigation supplies the independent source breakdown and focused render requirements; whether those models sound sufficiently strange, convincing or powerful remains a listening question ([voice implementation](../apps/impulse/impulse_processor.cpp); [generic Tone explanation](../libs/parameters/plugin_ui.hpp)).

MIDI reproducibility is another concrete concern: its variation seed includes the callback-local sample index, unlike the sequencer's absolute-position hash. Passing sequencer partition tests does not prove identical MIDI bounces at different buffers. The existing offline lab also starts from historical core defaults rather than the current plugin's Steady Start, and the factory alternatives chiefly vary rhythms/global macros after resetting the same per-object sounds. That can underrepresent timbral potential in both first-use and research. Prioritize repeatability, honest inactive/latched-control feedback, representative sound starting points and silent track selection before further synthesis engines. These findings remain high-priority backlog items even though the first three cross-family experiments target other products; Impulse is not being redesigned into another instrument ([MIDI seed and snapshots](../apps/impulse/impulse_processor.cpp); [presets and audition](../apps/impulse/impulse_plugin.cpp); [existing lab](../apps/dsp-lab/impulse.cpp)).

## Loop and Field must explain time and memory

### Loop's generation mechanism is real, its motion clock is wrong

Loop genuinely prints transformed playback into subsequent retained generations. That is a stronger differentiator than another static “tape” color. Its capture and gain relationships nevertheless require explanation: an empty capture records `input*(1−overdub)`, so the default midpoint attenuates new material and maximum Overdub records no new signal into an empty deck. Feedback acts during capture, not continuously on a merely playing tape. Record uses different gain conventions in reproduce and print paths; stronger settings can reduce monitored peaks while making later generations hotter. Loss affects reproduction and printing differently. These interactions can create meaningful evolution or make the process hard to predict; raw and matched multi-generation comparisons must distinguish the two ([capture, reproduction and print](../apps/loop/loop_processor.cpp); [Loop investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/loop_field.md)).

The selected Loop investigation isolates a less ambiguous fault. Its shared motion phase advances in seconds but wraps after one second; noninteger 0.47, 6.71 and 0.173 multiples therefore replay one-second fragments instead of completing their intended cycles. The nominal slow drift forcing never traverses its full sinusoid. A bounded 1000-second shared period makes those decimal-frequency cycles mathematically periodic without adding controls or state topology. Keep all motion amounts and signal laws unchanged for the comparison. Inspect long modulation traces, splice/reset artifacts, pitch movement and recorded audio; do not equate increased irregularity with better tape behavior. This candidate deliberately does not also solve the rate-dependent drift smoother, pitch-head/signed-speed interaction or fixed sample-sized windows ([motion and pitch code](../apps/loop/loop_processor.cpp); [experiment evidence](../docs/research/power/EVIDENCE.md)).

Current Loop **does save tapes and generations in schema 3**. Older control-only documentation is superseded and must not be reported as a current failure. Recall starts playback from tape beginning and omits an unfinished print destination; snapshot memory and off-thread restoration costs are explicit. “Host Sync” means tempo-derived duration, not automatic bar-quantized capture or PPQ phase lock. MIDI can perform capture/print/history actions, whereas GUI actions use bounded callback-boundary requests rather than ordinary automation lanes. A clearer capture/playback/print hierarchy and truthful endpoint/sync labels are smaller usability improvements than adding another transport mode ([state contract](../docs/products/loop/STATE_FORMAT.md); [recall decision](../docs/adr/0016-playable-defaults-and-tape-recall.md); [adapter](../apps/loop/loop_plugin.cpp)).

### Field holds a transforming network, not a lossless object

Field already has a substantial moving eight-line feedback network with damping, grain-like delay offsets, nonlinear writes and fixed fifth/octave pitch returns. FOREVER raises retention while also imposing extra Grain, Pitch and Motion influence, reducing input injection, and retaining damping/interpolation losses. It is not a lossless freeze, nor is it an independent captured sound object. During full hold, Mass stops changing the retention interpolation but continues changing damping. These choices can yield character; they also make a small number of controls change roles just when the player most needs predictable performance. Compare wet-only and matched mixed cases, then current hold against a narrowly retention-only candidate before concluding that more persistence or more pitch movement is desirable ([network and control laws](../apps/field/field_processor.cpp); [topology choices](../docs/adr/0015-field-stored-energy-topology.md)).

The first interface correction is semantic rather than decorative. “Stored Energy” is a smoothed square of the mono sum of line outputs, not total stored line energy; cancellation can reduce it while the buffers still contain material. “Retention” displays an untrimmed coefficient, not actual decay or the final feedback gain. Both are DSP-derived, but their labels overstate what they explain. Honest labels or a properly defined bounded observation can improve control without changing sound. The current footer already explains additional hold motion, and the Pitch tooltip correctly says return amount rather than tuning; the audit must not invent missing fixes. What remains unresolved is how easily a person can predict those transformations, release them and recover a performance ([meters and UI](../apps/field/field_plugin.cpp); [actual interface review](../docs/research/power/INTERFACE.md)).

Field's saved state contains controls, not the particular live energy that made a held performance interesting. That is a genuine workflow boundary, different from a judgment that the reverb sounds weak. A future energy snapshot requires explicit state/memory ownership and migration, not silently changing schema 1. Likewise, alternate pitch-return intervals are a focused research possibility, not permission for an algorithm menu. Preserve the current architecture as a control until listening establishes whether its upward harmonic bias adds identity or limits useful contexts ([state format](../docs/products/field/STATE_FORMAT.md); [Field investigation](../research_notes/Aste%20musical%20power%20audit/loop_field.md)).

## Three investigations precede an Aste 2.0 decision

The first selection is **confidence-weighted and provisional**, not a proven ranking of musical benefit. Sequence retriggering has a direct action-to-envelope failure and a small causal intervention. Loop's motion period has a direct mathematical mismatch and can be isolated from its other tape laws. Harmonic's linked gain experiment tests whether useful capability already exists but is hidden by operating point, avoiding premature DSP replacement. Density's overlapping control laws and Field's hold semantics are important but less reducible to a single obvious correction; Impulse's inactive control and MIDI seed ownership remain explicit high-priority engineering findings. Full methods, negative controls, actual results and retained rejected candidates belong in the evidence ledger, not in retrospective claims that every alternative succeeded ([evidence](../docs/research/power/EVIDENCE.md); [backlog](../docs/research/power/BACKLOG.md)).

The family-level direction should be **more accountable gestures, not more machinery**. Preserve the dark technical visual identity, direct numerical editing and visible structures. Let controls communicate when they engage, whether they affect current sound or the next event, and what their feedback actually measures. Distinguish intentional cuts from unintended discontinuities, evolving memory from merely long tails, and spectral change from output gain. Existing actual-editor images show coherent family design; the remaining work is hierarchy, truthful dependencies, precise grid manipulation, preset identity and native interaction evidence, not skeuomorphism or decorative science ([screenshots and interaction analysis](../docs/research/power/INTERFACE.md)).

Musical acceptance should combine matched listening with a repeatable composition task. A person must deliberately create restrained and radical-but-useful outcomes, predict a gesture, reproduce it, automate it and reopen the session. Preference, distinctiveness, perceived control, time/edits and errors should be recorded separately. The listening material must span sparse/dense percussion, bass, oscillators, noise, chords and complex musical passages, with raw as well as matched outputs. Procedural excerpts are controlled fixtures, not substitutes for the creator's projects. Where an effect's matching trim could activate its protection, match outside the graph. Where a matched file hits a safety ceiling, disclose the achieved level rather than claiming equal loudness. “No useful difference” is a valid outcome, as is discovering that an existing control gesture already solves the problem ([listening policy](../docs/testing/LISTENING.md); [acceptance tasks](../docs/research/power/BACKLOG.md)).

## Conclusion

Aste 2.0 should not yet be a six-engine rewrite. The present evidence supports correcting lost intent, exposing operating conditions and then making a small number of musically selected changes under explicit compatibility boundaries. A larger engine becomes justified only when the current instrument cannot achieve a valued result reliably—not when the implementation offers room for another feature.

The decisive next evidence is whether the creator can use these controls to make and repeat music that the present workflow frustrates. Engineering measurements make that comparison trustworthy; they cannot supply the preference or identity judgment. Until those judgments exist, the honest outcome is a sharper diagnosis and a reproducible set of candidates, not a declaration that the instruments are fixed.
